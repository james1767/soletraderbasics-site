# UK Sole Trader Tax & Admin Calendar 2026/27

This calendar brings together the main UK sole trader tax dates and useful bookkeeping/admin reminders for the 2026/27 tax year.

Dates labelled **HMRC date** are statutory or official HMRC dates. Dates labelled **SoleTraderBasics reminder** are practical housekeeping prompts, not legal deadlines.

This is a general resource, not personalised tax advice. If your situation is complex, check GOV.UK or speak to a qualified adviser.

## Making Tax Digital for Income Tax timetable

MTD for Income Tax is being introduced in stages. GOV.UK guidance says you need to use it if all of the following apply: you are a sole trader or landlord registered for Self Assessment, you get income from self-employment or property, and your qualifying income is above the relevant threshold.

| Tax return used to check qualifying income | Qualifying income threshold | MTD start date |
|---|---:|---|
| 2024/25 | More than £50,000 | 6 April 2026 |
| 2025/26 | More than £30,000 | 6 April 2027 |
| 2026/27 | More than £20,000 | 6 April 2028 |

Qualifying income is not the same thing as profit after expenses. HMRC reviews Self Assessment returns and says it will write to people who need to start using MTD, but it remains your responsibility to check if and when the rules apply to you.

If you are within MTD, you may need compatible software to keep digital records, send quarterly updates to HMRC, and submit your tax return through software. If you are not within MTD, do not assume the quarterly update dates apply to you.

Official guidance: [find out if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax).

## Monthly sole trader admin checklist

These are SoleTraderBasics practical suggestions, not HMRC statutory deadlines. Pick a regular point in the month that fits your work.

- Reconcile business bank transactions.
- Record business income.
- Capture and organise receipts.
- Categorise business expenses.
- Check unpaid invoices.
- Maintain bookkeeping records.
- Review cash available for future tax bills.
- Back up your records.

## Quarterly review checklist

This is a voluntary business review, not the same thing as statutory MTD quarterly updates.

- Check year-to-date turnover.
- Review profit after expenses.
- Estimate likely tax and National Insurance.
- Check money set aside for future tax bills.
- Review overdue invoices.
- Look for missing receipts or unclear expense notes.
- Check whether MTD might apply now or in a future tax year.

  <h2 id="record-retention-heading">How long to keep sole trader records</h2>
  <p>GOV.UK says self-employed people must keep records for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline for the relevant tax year. For 2026/27, that means keeping normal Self Assessment business records until at least 31 January 2033.</p>
  <p>If you file very late or HMRC opens a check, you may need to keep records for longer. For a simple breakdown, read <a href="/guides/accounting-software/sole-trader-record-keeping-requirements/">sole trader record keeping requirements</a>.</p>
